Keto Drinks

Drinks are an easy place to slip up on keto, because sugar hides in juice, fizzy drinks, flavoured coffees, and alcohol. The good news is that the best keto drinks are simple and cheap: water, tea, and coffee. This guide covers what to drink, what to avoid, and how to handle alcohol and sweet cravings without breaking ketosis.

Water and hydration

Water is the most important drink on keto. When you cut carbs, your body releases stored water and excretes more sodium, so you need to drink more than before — aim for at least two litres a day, and more in hot weather or after exercise. Sparkling water with a squeeze of lemon or lime is a refreshing change, and it can replace fizzy drinks without any sugar. Herbal teas, such as peppermint, chamomile, and rooibos, are naturally caffeine-free and carb-free. Keep a water bottle with you and sip through the day rather than waiting until you feel thirsty.

Coffee and tea

Black coffee and plain tea are both keto-friendly, with almost no carbs. If you like your coffee creamy, add double cream, a little butter, or unsweetened almond milk. Bulletproof coffee — coffee blended with butter and coconut oil — is popular as a breakfast replacement, but it is high in calories, so use it deliberately rather than by habit. Avoid flavoured lattes, sweetened iced teas, and coffee drinks with syrups, which can contain more sugar than a dessert. Green tea is a good choice too, and it contains compounds that may support metabolism.

Alcohol on keto

Alcohol is not forbidden on keto, but it needs care. Your tolerance is usually lower on keto, and alcohol pauses fat burning while your liver processes it. Spirits such as vodka, gin, whisky, and tequila contain no carbs, so a spirit with soda water and lime is the safest choice. Dry wine has about 2 to 3 grams of carbs per glass, while beer is much higher at 10 to 15 grams per pint and is best avoided. Sweet cocktails, liqueurs, and mixers like tonic and cola are full of sugar. If you drink, eat first, drink water between drinks, and stop after one or two.

Drinks to avoid

The biggest drinks to avoid are the obvious ones: fizzy drinks, fruit juice, sports drinks, energy drinks, and sweetened iced tea. A single glass of orange juice can contain 20 grams of sugar, which is a full day of carbs on keto. Flavoured waters often contain sugar too, so check the label. Diet fizzy drinks have no sugar, but they keep a sweet tooth alive and some people find they trigger cravings, so use them sparingly. Milk is higher in carbs than cream, so use cream or unsweetened plant milk in coffee instead.

Making keto drinks at home

Homemade keto drinks are cheaper and healthier than anything you can buy. Iced coffee is easy: brew coffee, let it cool, and pour it over ice with a splash of cream and a few drops of stevia. Flavoured water is just as simple — add cucumber and mint, or lemon and ginger, to a jug of cold water and leave it in the fridge for an hour. A keto smoothie with avocado, spinach, almond milk, and protein powder makes a quick breakfast or a post-workout drink. For an evening treat, mix sparkling water with a squeeze of lime and a few mint leaves for a drink that feels like a cocktail without the sugar or alcohol. These drinks take minutes to make and they keep you away from sugary alternatives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drink diet soda on keto?

Diet soda has no sugar or carbs, so it will not break ketosis. Many people use it occasionally, but water, tea, and sparkling water are better everyday choices.

Does alcohol kick you out of ketosis?

Alcohol pauses fat burning while it is being processed, but it does not directly stop ketosis. The bigger risks are lower tolerance, poor food choices, and the carbs in beer and sweet mixers.

Is bulletproof coffee good for weight loss?

Not necessarily. It is very high in calories, so it can stall weight loss if you drink it on top of normal meals. It works best as a meal replacement, not an extra.
