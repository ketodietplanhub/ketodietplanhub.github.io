Keto Foods List

Knowing exactly which foods fit keto makes shopping and cooking far easier. This guide lists the foods that work well, the ones that are fine in small amounts, and the ones that will quickly push you over your carb limit. Keep it as a reference when you plan meals or stand in the supermarket wondering whether something belongs in your basket.

Proteins: meat, fish, and eggs

Animal proteins are the backbone of keto because they are naturally very low in carbs. Beef, lamb, pork, chicken, turkey, and duck are all suitable, and fattier cuts are often a better fit than lean ones because they provide the fat keto relies on. Oily fish such as salmon, mackerel, sardines, and trout are excellent choices, since they add omega-3 fats as well as protein. Eggs are one of the best keto foods of all: cheap, versatile, and almost carb-free. Choose unprocessed meat where you can, and check sausages and cured meats for added sugar or starch.

Fats and oils

Fat is your main energy source on keto, so choose good ones. Olive oil, avocado oil, coconut oil, butter, ghee, and lard are all useful in the kitchen. Avocados, olives, nuts, and seeds provide fat along with fibre and nutrients. Nuts are keto-friendly in small portions, but macadamias, pecans, and Brazil nuts are lower in carbs than cashews, which are easy to overeat. Avoid industrial seed oils that are heavily processed, and skip anything labelled low-fat, because the fat is usually replaced with sugar or starch.

Vegetables and dairy

Above-ground vegetables are generally the lowest in carbs: leafy greens, broccoli, cauliflower, courgette, aubergine, peppers, mushrooms, green beans, and asparagus. Root vegetables such as potatoes, carrots, parsnips, and beetroot are much higher in carbs and are best avoided or used sparingly. Full-fat dairy fits keto well: hard cheeses, cream cheese, double cream, butter, and plain Greek yoghurt. Milk is higher in sugar than cream, so use cream in coffee and keep milk portions small. Herbs and spices add flavour for almost no carbs.

Foods to avoid

Bread, pasta, rice, oats, breakfast cereals, and flour-based foods are all too high in carbs for keto. The same goes for potatoes, sweet potatoes, corn, peas, and most beans and lentils. Sugar in all its forms is out, including honey, maple syrup, and agave, and so are fruit juice, fizzy drinks, and beer. Most fruit is too sugary, with the exception of small servings of berries. Watch out for sauces and condiments: ketchup, barbecue sauce, and many salad dressings contain surprising amounts of sugar.

Shopping with the list

Take this list to the shop and use it as your guide for the first month. Start in the fresh food sections — meat, fish, eggs, and vegetables — and only visit the middle aisles for pantry items such as oils, nuts, and tinned goods. Read the label on anything in a packet, and check the carbohydrate content per 100 grams, which is the most useful number for comparing products. If a food is not on the list, look it up before you buy it, because a single high-carb item can use up your whole day's allowance. Over time you will know your regular foods by heart, and shopping will become quick and automatic, but in the beginning the list is your best friend.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are tomatoes keto-friendly?

Yes, in moderation. Tomatoes contain about 3 to 4 grams of net carbs per 100 grams, so a small amount in a salad or sauce is fine, but a large portion can add up.

Can I drink milk on keto?

Milk contains lactose, a natural sugar, at around 5 grams of carbs per 100 ml. Small amounts are possible, but cream or unsweetened almond milk is usually a better choice.

Is peanut butter allowed on keto?

Natural peanut butter with no added sugar is fine in small amounts, at about 3 to 4 grams of net carbs per tablespoon. Check the label, because many brands add sugar.
