Keto Shopping List

A good keto shopping list makes the weekly shop faster, cheaper, and far less tempting. This guide gives you a complete list organised by section of the shop, the pantry staples worth keeping, and the items that look keto-friendly but are not. Print it, save it, or use it to plan your next shop.

Meat, fish, and eggs

Start with your proteins. Beef mince, chicken thighs, pork chops, and lamb are versatile and affordable, and fattier cuts suit keto better than lean ones. Oily fish such as salmon, mackerel, and sardines are excellent, and tinned tuna is a useful standby. Eggs are a must — buy them by the dozen. Add bacon, sausages, and cured meats, but check the label for added sugar and starch. If you eat dairy, include butter, hard cheese, cream cheese, and double cream. A rotisserie chicken is a great shortcut for busy weeks.

Vegetables, fruit, and dairy

Fill your basket with above-ground vegetables: leafy greens, spinach, broccoli, cauliflower, courgette, peppers, mushrooms, green beans, and asparagus. Avocados are a keto staple, so buy several at different stages of ripeness. Lemons and limes add flavour for almost no carbs, and a small punnet of berries is the only fruit most keto shoppers need. In the dairy aisle, choose full-fat options: Greek yoghurt, cream, and hard cheeses. Skip low-fat products, which usually contain added sugar or starch to replace the fat.

Pantry staples

A well-stocked keto pantry makes cooking easy. Keep olive oil, coconut oil, and butter for cooking, and apple cider vinegar, soy sauce, mustard, and hot sauce for flavour. Almond flour and coconut flour are the baking essentials, along with baking powder, vanilla extract, and cocoa powder. Stock erythritol or monk fruit sweetener, chia seeds, flaxseed, and a selection of nuts and seeds. Keep tinned tomatoes, coconut milk, and stock cubes for quick sauces and soups. Herbs and spices — garlic, ginger, cumin, paprika, oregano — turn plain ingredients into proper meals.

What to leave on the shelf

The items to skip are the ones that fill most supermarket aisles: bread, pasta, rice, cereals, crackers, and biscuits. Avoid potatoes, sweet potatoes, and corn, and skip fruit juice, fizzy drinks, and beer. Be cautious with sauces and dressings — many contain sugar — and with low-carb or keto-branded products, which are often expensive and full of sweeteners. Read the label on anything processed: if sugar, maltodextrin, or starch appears in the first few ingredients, put it back. Shopping the edges of the store, where fresh food lives, is the simplest rule.

A weekly shop in practice

Here is how the shopping list works in a real week. On Saturday, plan your meals and write the list. On Sunday, do the main shop: meat and fish for the week, vegetables, eggs, and dairy. On Wednesday, do a small top-up shop for fresh vegetables and salad, since they do not last a full week. Freeze any meat you will not cook within three days, and keep a few tinned and frozen backups for busy nights. At the end of the week, check what is left before you write the next list, so you buy less of what you wasted and more of what you used. After a few weeks, the shop becomes routine, and you will know roughly what it costs and how long it takes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the five keto essentials to always have at home?

Eggs, butter, olive oil, leafy greens, and a protein such as chicken or mince. With those five, you can make a keto meal any day.

Is almond flour worth the price?

It is expensive, but a bag lasts a long time because you use small amounts. It is worth it if you bake, but not essential — many keto meals need no flour at all.

Can I buy ready-made keto meals?

Some shops sell them, but they are pricey and often high in sweeteners or additives. Cooking simple keto meals at home is cheaper and usually healthier.
