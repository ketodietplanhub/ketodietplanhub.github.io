Keto Flu

Keto flu is the nickname for the cluster of symptoms some people get in the first week of a keto diet: headache, fatigue, irritability, brain fog, muscle cramps, and nausea. It is not a real flu, and it is not dangerous for most people, but it can be unpleasant enough to make people quit. This guide explains why it happens and how to avoid it.

Why keto flu happens

When you cut carbs, your body uses up its stored glucose, and each gram of stored glucose releases water with it. The result is a rapid loss of water and electrolytes — sodium, potassium, and magnesium — in the first few days. At the same time, insulin levels fall, which makes the kidneys excrete even more sodium. This sudden shift in fluids and minerals is what causes the headache, tiredness, and cramps. It is a sign that your body is adapting, not that something is wrong, but it is uncomfortable and worth preventing.

Symptoms and how long they last

The most common symptoms are headache, fatigue, dizziness, irritability, difficulty concentrating, muscle cramps, and trouble sleeping. Some people also feel nauseous or have digestive changes. Symptoms usually start two to four days after cutting carbs and last for three to seven days. Most people feel better by the end of the first week, and many feel better than before once they are through it. If symptoms are severe, last more than two weeks, or include a high fever or vomiting, seek medical advice, because that is not normal keto flu.

Prevention: electrolytes and water

The best prevention is to replace what you lose. Drink plenty of water — at least two litres a day — and add extra salt to your food in the first week. A cup of salty broth once or twice a day is an easy way to replace sodium. Eat potassium-rich foods such as leafy greens, avocado, mushrooms, and salmon. Some people take a magnesium supplement in the evening, which can also help with sleep and cramps, but check with your doctor first. Start these habits on day one, before symptoms appear, rather than waiting until you feel unwell.

Treating keto flu

If keto flu hits, the fastest fix is usually salt and water: drink a large glass of water with a pinch of salt, or have a cup of bone broth. Rest, avoid intense exercise for a few days, and eat a proper keto meal with enough fat and protein. A warm bath with Epsom salts can ease muscle cramps. Avoid the temptation to eat a large plate of carbs to feel better, since that will undo the adaptation and you will have to go through it again. Most people find that one or two days of good hydration and rest is enough to turn the corner.

What to eat in the first week

Food choices in the first week can make keto flu much easier. Eat plenty of salty foods: broth, pickles, olives, and salted nuts all help replace sodium. Include potassium-rich foods such as leafy greens, avocado, mushrooms, and salmon at most meals. Cook with butter and olive oil so your meals are satisfying, and do not try to restrict calories in the first week — eat enough to feel full while your body adjusts. Keep meals simple: eggs and spinach for breakfast, chicken salad for lunch, and salmon with broccoli for dinner. Avoid alcohol in the first week, since it worsens dehydration, and drink water steadily through the day rather than in large amounts at once.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does keto flu last?

Usually three to seven days, starting two to four days after you cut carbs. Most people feel normal again by the end of the first week.

Can I prevent keto flu?

Yes, largely. Drink plenty of water, add extra salt to your food, eat potassium-rich foods, and consider magnesium. Start on day one.

Is keto flu dangerous?

For most healthy people it is uncomfortable but not dangerous. Seek medical advice if symptoms are severe, last more than two weeks, or include vomiting or a high fever.
