Keto for Beginners

The ketogenic diet, usually just called keto, is a way of eating that is very low in carbohydrates, moderate in protein, and high in fat. When carbs are scarce, the body switches from burning glucose to burning fat and producing ketones for energy. That switch is called ketosis, and it is the whole point of the diet. This guide explains how keto works, what a typical day looks like, and how to start without making the common early mistakes.

How keto works

Your body normally runs on glucose, which comes mainly from carbohydrates such as bread, rice, potatoes, and sugar. When you cut carbs to roughly 20 to 50 grams of net carbs a day, glucose stores run low and the liver starts converting fat into ketones, which the brain and muscles can use as fuel. This metabolic state is called ketosis. It usually takes two to four days of very low carb eating to enter ketosis, and it can take a few more weeks before your body uses fat efficiently. During that adjustment period many people feel tired or foggy, which is normal and usually passes as the body adapts and as you replace lost fluids and electrolytes.

What to eat on keto

The foundation of keto is real, minimally processed food. Good choices include meat, poultry, fish, eggs, butter, olive oil, coconut oil, avocados, nuts, seeds, and above-ground vegetables such as leafy greens, broccoli, cauliflower, courgette, and peppers. Full-fat dairy like cheese, cream, and Greek yoghurt fits well in moderation. Foods to avoid are the obvious carb sources: bread, pasta, rice, cereals, potatoes, most fruit, sugar, honey, and sweetened drinks. Read labels carefully, because sugar hides in sauces, dressings, and processed meats, and even small amounts can add up over a day.

A typical day on keto

Breakfast might be eggs fried in butter with spinach and avocado. Lunch could be a large salad with grilled chicken, olive oil dressing, cheese, and nuts. Dinner might be salmon with buttered broccoli or a steak with cauliflower mash. Snacks, if you want them, can be a handful of almonds, a piece of cheese, or celery with cream cheese. Drink plenty of water and consider adding a little extra salt to your food in the first week, because keto makes the body excrete more sodium. Many people find their appetite drops naturally after the first week, which is one reason the diet helps some people lose weight.

Starting safely

Before starting keto, it is worth talking to your doctor, especially if you take medication for diabetes or blood pressure, are pregnant, or have kidney or liver disease. Start by clearing your kitchen of high-carb temptations and stocking up on keto-friendly foods so you are not caught hungry with nothing suitable to eat. Track your carbs for the first two weeks using a food diary or an app, because it is easy to underestimate them. Expect the first week to feel strange, plan simple meals, and be patient with yourself while your body adapts to its new fuel source.

Your first week on keto

The first week is the hardest part, so keep it simple and do not try to be perfect. Plan three easy breakfasts, three lunches, and three dinners, and repeat them through the week so you always know what you are eating. Drink plenty of water, add salt to your food, and keep keto snacks ready for moments of hunger. Weigh and track your food for these first seven days, because it teaches you faster than any guide what your carb limit looks like in practice. Expect some tiredness and a headache around day three, and remember that it passes. By the end of the week, most people feel steadier, less hungry, and more confident, and the diet starts to feel like a routine rather than a challenge.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to get into ketosis?

Most people enter ketosis within two to four days of eating under about 50 grams of net carbs a day, though full adaptation to burning fat efficiently can take three to six weeks.

Can I eat fruit on keto?

Most fruit is too high in sugar for keto, but small portions of berries such as raspberries, strawberries, and blackberries can fit if you count the carbs carefully.

Is keto safe for everyone?

Keto is not suitable for everyone. People with certain medical conditions or on certain medications should get medical advice before starting, and children and pregnant women should not follow a strict keto diet without supervision.
