Keto Travel Tips

Travelling on keto can feel daunting, but it is very doable with a little preparation. Airports, motorway services, hotels, and restaurants all have keto options if you know what to look for. This guide covers packing snacks, eating out, surviving flights, and enjoying your holiday without abandoning your diet.

Packing keto snacks

Before you travel, pack a small bag of keto snacks: nuts, cheese, beef jerky, pork scratchings, and a few boiled eggs if the journey is short. Nut butter sachets are handy and travel well. A reusable water bottle is essential, since dehydration is common when travelling and keto increases your need for water. If you are flying, remember that liquids are restricted, so pack solid snacks and buy water after security. Having your own snacks means you are never forced to buy whatever is available at a service station.

Eating out and at hotels

At restaurants, order grilled meat or fish with salad or vegetables instead of bread, rice, or chips, and ask for butter or olive oil on the side. Most kitchens are happy to swap sides. At a hotel breakfast, choose eggs, bacon, cheese, and yoghurt, and skip the toast, pastries, and cereal. If your hotel has a fridge, stock it with cheese, cold meat, and salad from a nearby shop. When eating with friends or at a buffet, fill your plate with the protein and vegetable options first, and enjoy the meal without worrying about perfection.

Flights and long journeys

Airline meals are usually high in carbs, so eat before you fly or order a special meal in advance — many airlines offer a low-carb or diabetic meal if you ask. On long flights, drink plenty of water and bring your own snacks. On road trips, stop at supermarkets rather than fast-food restaurants; a rotisserie chicken and a bag of salad from a supermarket is a proper keto meal. If you do stop at a fast-food place, order a burger without the bun or a grilled chicken salad. Plan your stops in advance when you can, so you are not making hungry decisions.

Staying flexible on holiday

Holidays are for enjoying yourself, and a rigid approach can spoil them. Decide your approach before you go: some people stay strictly keto, others allow a few higher-carb meals and return to keto when they get home. Either is fine, as long as it is a choice rather than a slide. If you do eat carbs, keep portions modest, drink plenty of water, and get back to keto the next day rather than waiting until Monday. A short break does not undo months of progress, and the diet will still be there when you get home.

A keto travel checklist

Before any trip, run through a short checklist. Pack snacks: nuts, cheese, jerky, and nut butter sachets. Pack a reusable water bottle and a small bag of salt, since keto increases your need for both. Download a map of supermarkets near your hotel, so you know where to buy food. Check the restaurant menus at your destination, and note two or three places with keto-friendly options. If you are flying, eat before you go and carry snacks in your hand luggage. If you are staying with friends or family, tell them in advance that you eat low-carb, and offer to cook a meal for everyone. A little preparation turns a stressful trip into an easy one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What snacks can I take on a plane?

Nuts, cheese, beef jerky, pork scratchings, and keto bars are all fine in hand luggage. Buy water after security and avoid the sugary snacks on board.

Can I stay keto at an all-inclusive hotel?

Yes, usually. Buffets have grilled meats, fish, salads, eggs, and cheese. Skip the bread, rice, pasta, and desserts, and you can eat very well.

What if I eat carbs while travelling?

Do not panic. Enjoy the meal, drink water, and return to keto at your next meal. One higher-carb meal does not undo your progress.
